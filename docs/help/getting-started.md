# Getting started

With {appName} you make a website on your own computer and then export it for your web host. Your site is kept in one file (ending `.wforge`) that you own. The website it makes is a set of plain files that work on any web host, with nothing loaded from anywhere else.

A site is made of **pages**. Each page is a stack of **sections**, such as a Hero, a Gallery or a Contact form. Each section holds **elements**, such as headings, text, pictures and buttons.

## Starting {appName}

Open {appName} from its icon, the letters **WF** in front of a flame on an anvil: in the Start menu on Windows, or in Applications on a Mac. Double-clicking a site's `.wforge` file opens {appName} too.

While {appName} starts, its window says **Starting {appName}…**. Then you see the site you opened, such as one you double-clicked, or the start screen. If something goes wrong while it starts, the window says so: choose **Try again**. Your work is saved.

## The start screen

When no site is open, you see the start screen. On the left are {appName}'s icon and name, and the version you have.

- **Start a site…** asks a few questions about your business and makes a site that's already yours. This is the quickest way to begin.
- **Choose a template yourself…** lets you pick a starting design from the full list.
- **Open a site…** opens a site you've saved on this computer.
- **Try an example** opens the finished Café / Restaurant site to look around and change. {appName} asks where to save your copy.

On the right, **Recent sites** lists the sites you've opened, newest first.

- **Search sites** finds one by its name, file name or folder.
- The pin keeps a site at the top of the list.
- The **×** takes a site off the list. It doesn't delete the site's file.

The **Help** tab lists the help pages to start with (this one, _The editor_ and _Keyboard shortcuts_), then the keyboard shortcuts and the example site.

## Start a site (the quick start)

Four short steps. Every question is optional, you can go back to any step, and **Create site** works from any step. You can change every answer later.

1. **Your business:** what kind of business it is (Food and drink, Beauty, Trades, Services, Creative, Shop or Personal), its name, and one line on what you do. Search engines show that line under your site's name. While [the assistant](assistant.md) is on, it also asks you to **Tell us about your business** in a few sentences: see [Writing your pages' words](assistant.md#writing-your-pages-words).
2. **A look:** a theme, and a template suited to your kind of business. For Food and drink or Trades, you can open a finished example site instead.
3. **Pages:** choose **Separate pages** or **One page**, which puts everything on your home page with a menu that scrolls to each section (see [One-page sites](one-page-sites.md)). Untick a template page to leave it out. **More pages for** your business type suggests extra pages, such as _Reviews_ or _Areas we cover_.
4. **Colours and details:** keep the theme's colours, pick your own brand colour, or choose **From my logo…** to take colours from your logo. {appName} reads the logo on this computer and doesn't add it to your site. Then add your email, phone number, address, opening hours and web address, if you have them.

{appName} asks where to save the site's file, then opens it. Your answers replace the template's sample business wherever it appeared: in the text, the Header, the Footer and [Site Settings](site-settings.md). Anything you skipped keeps the template's sample text. [Site Check](site-check.md) points that text out before you export.

## The tour

The first time a site you made with **Start a site…** opens, {appName} offers a short tour. Choose **Show me around**, or **Not now** to start working straight away. It isn't offered again either way, and **Help › Take the Tour** starts it at any time.

The tour has three steps, each in a small card beside the button or text it's about:

1. **Change your words:** click any text on your page and type. The tour moves on once you've changed something.
2. **See it as visitors will:** [Preview](preview.md).
3. **Put it online:** [Publish](publish.md).

A last card says where help is. Each card has **Back**, **Next**, **Skip tour** and a **Learn more** link to its help page. The cards never get in your way: you can keep working while one is open, and **Esc** closes the tour. On the keyboard, Tab moves from a card's buttons on to the rest of the window, and F6 moves between the card and the other parts of the window.

## First-time tips

The first time you reach some parts of {appName}, a short tip says what's there. Each tip shows once. **Got it** closes it, and it closes by itself as you carry on. A tip never takes you away from what you're doing; F6 reaches it from the keyboard:

- the first time you **select** something on the page: how to change it;
- the first time you switch to **Content mode**: what it locks;
- the first **Must fix** in [Site Check](site-check.md): that it stops Export and Publish until it's fixed;
- after your first **export**: that your website is a folder to upload, as it is;
- after you first close **Theme Studio**: that a theme changes every page, and Undo takes it back.

To see no more tips, turn off **Show tips** in [Settings](settings.md#interface). **Show the tour and tips again** there starts over.

## Choose a template yourself

**New Site** (Ctrl+N on Windows, Cmd+N on a Mac) shows every template.

- Give the site a **Site name** and choose a **Theme**.
- **Start from scratch** gives you a _Blank site_ with Home, About and Contact pages.
- Under **Templates**, filter by **Business type**, sort, and click **Details** for a larger look.
- Choosing a template also chooses its theme. You can change the theme straight after, or at any time.

See [Templates](templates.md) for the list.

## What next

1. Click any text on the page and type. See [Editing on the page](editing-on-the-page.md).
2. Change pictures in [Media](media.md), and colours and fonts in the [Theme](themes.md).
3. Add [pages](pages.md) and [sections](sections.md).
4. [Preview](preview.md) your site in your browser and on your phone.
5. Fix anything [Site Check](site-check.md) finds, then [export](export.md) your site for your web host.

{appName} saves as you work. See [Saving, copies and backups](saving-and-backups.md).

## Updates

On Windows, {appName} keeps itself up to date. A few seconds after it starts, and every six hours while it's open, it looks for a new version. If there is one, it downloads it in the background while you work, and says so when it's ready: "{appName} 1.2.0 is ready: it installs when you close {appName}." The next time you close {appName}, the new version installs, and it's there when you open {appName} again. Nothing interrupts you, and your sites aren't changed.

{appName} looks for new versions on GitHub, where they're published. It sends nothing about you or your sites. Without an internet connection it tries again later.

Each new version reaches **Beta** first, then **Stable**. Until {appName} 1.0, {appName} gets its updates from Beta. To choose, see [Settings](settings.md#updates).

On a Mac, {appName} doesn't update itself yet: install each new version yourself.
