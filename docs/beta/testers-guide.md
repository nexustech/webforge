# Webforge beta: a guide for testers

Thank you for helping us test Webforge before it's released. Webforge makes a website for your business on your own computer, without needing to know how websites work. In the beta, a small group of business owners, on Windows and on Mac, use it for real and tell us what gets in their way.

## What you need

- **On Windows:** Windows 10 or 11.
- **On a Mac:** a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later), with macOS 13 or later. The beta doesn't run on Macs with an Intel processor.

## What we'd like you to do

Make a real site for your business, or a copy of the one you have, and use Webforge as you would if it were finished. Along the way, try to:

- start a site from a template, or with **Start a site…** and a few questions about your business;
- change the words and pictures, and add a page or two;
- choose your colours and fonts with a theme;
- check the site with **Preview**, on your computer and on your phone;
- run **Site Check** and fix what it finds;
- publish the site to your web host, or export it as files;
- save a version, change something, and go back to the version.

You don't need to do it all at once. A little every week tells us more than one long go.

## Keeping your work safe

This is a test version, so keep a copy of anything you'd be sorry to lose:

- **File › Save a Copy as ZIP…** saves your whole site as one file. Keep it somewhere else, such as a USB stick or your cloud folder.
- **File › Save Version…** keeps the site as it is now, to go back to later.

Each site is one file ending `.wforge`, wherever you saved it. A new beta version opens your sites, and you carry on with them as before. Once it has saved a site, an older version can only open that site read-only, so keep to the newest version.

## Finding your version

Choose **Help › About Webforge**. It shows the version, for example _Version 0.9.0_. Every report asks for it, since things change from week to week.

## Telling us what you found

We collect reports on GitHub, through two short forms. You need a free GitHub account to send one.

- **[Something went wrong](https://github.com/nexustech/webforge/issues/new?template=beta-bug.yml):** Webforge did something it shouldn't, showed an error, or closed unexpectedly.
- **[Feedback or an idea](https://github.com/nexustech/webforge/issues/new?template=beta-feedback.yml):** something was confusing, slow or missing, or you'd like Webforge to do something it doesn't.

If you'd rather not use GitHub, reply to the email that invited you, with the same details.

What helps most:

- what you were trying to do, in your own words;
- what you expected, and what happened instead;
- the steps that led to it, if you remember them;
- a screenshot.

**Everything on GitHub can be read by anyone.** Leave out passwords, your customers' details and anything else private, and check a screenshot shows nothing private before adding it.

## If Webforge closes unexpectedly

Your work is saved as you go, so it's still there when you open Webforge again. Webforge also saves a crash report on your computer. It's never sent anywhere by itself, and it holds none of your site's content or personal details. Attaching it to your report helps us find the problem. [Sharing a crash report](../help/saving-and-backups.md#sharing-a-crash-report) says how.

## What happens next

We read every report each week and sort it by how much it stops people making their site. We reply on the report itself, and GitHub emails you when we do. When a fix is in a new beta version, we say so there.

## Getting help

Webforge's own help is under **Help › Webforge Help** (F1), and works without an internet connection. The same pages are in [docs/help](../help/README.md).
