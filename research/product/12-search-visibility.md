# Being found: search engines and the exported site

**Status:** ✅ Decided (SEO-1…SEO-12, product owner, 28 Oct 2026): all twelve adopted as proposed (§6). 3.5 had merged the same morning ([#548](https://github.com/nexustech/webforge/pull/548)), so SEO-1…SEO-4, SEO-8 and SEO-12 become item 3.15 ([#575](https://github.com/nexustech/webforge/issues/575), 4 days, stream B) rather than scope on 3.5; SEO-6 is 3.16 ([#576](https://github.com/nexustech/webforge/issues/576), 1.5 days, stream B) and SEO-7 is 3.17 ([#577](https://github.com/nexustech/webforge/issues/577), 1.5 days, stream C); SEO-9 adds 0.5 day to 3.11; +7.5 days in all. SEO-5 and SEO-10 stay v1.1, SEO-11 is a rule and SEO-12 answers MVP §7 item 12. The product owner's question: what Webforge has planned for search engine optimisation, and what the gaps are.

**Goal:** a small business's exported site should be as findable as the same site made in Wix or Squarespace, without Webforge growing an "SEO panel" of controls that Google itself says do nothing. The owner is not an SEO specialist; the builder gets the mechanics right from the details they have already typed, and tells them the two things only they can do (a Google Business Profile, and real content).

## 2. What search engines actually use

Google's documentation is unusually explicit about what matters and what does not. The points a site builder can act on:

| What | Google's guidance | For Webforge |
|---|---|---|
| **Indexable HTML** | Content, titles, links and canonicals in the initial HTML; `<a href>` links; every page reachable from another; nothing essential behind JavaScript or a click | The export is flat HTML with real links (X1); entrance animations keep content visible without script (F-DS-5). Already right |
| **One title per page** | Unique, descriptive, concise; the first heading should match it; brand briefly ("Page – Site"); avoid "Home". **No character limit**; Google truncates to the device and rewrites titles that are boilerplate or mismatch the page | The export writes *Page \| Site name* and the site name alone on the home page (3.3). Right, apart from a check that the page has one main heading |
| **One description per page** | Used when it describes the page better than Google's own snippet; unique per page; **no length limit**; keyword lists hurt | Page description falls back to the site's (1.18). Right; a search-result preview would help people write one |
| **Business details that match** | Local ranking is relevance, distance and prominence, fed mainly by the **Google Business Profile**; the site's name, address, phone and hours should match it exactly; `LocalBusiness` structured data (name, address, hours, phone, url, image) makes the page eligible for the knowledge panel and type-of-business carousels; never `aggregateRating` for your own business | Site Settings hold every field already (contact, opening hours, social profiles); nothing emits structured data. **The main gap** |
| **Mobile first, no interstitials** | Google indexes the mobile page; same content and metadata on every width; no intrusive popups | Responsive by construction; the consent banner is small and the announcement popup is v1.1 |
| **Images** | `<img>` with `srcset` and a fallback; alt text is the most important image metadata; explicit dimensions; lazy below the fold only; `og:image` per page | `<picture>` with WebP and a fallback, `srcset`, width and height, lazy below the fold, eager above (3.4); alt text stored once in Media (G15); share picture per page. Already right |
| **Core Web Vitals** | The only page-experience signal named as used by ranking; good scores do not guarantee anything; chasing a perfect score "may not be worth the effort" | Small CSS, one deferred script, no third parties (C8); 5.1's performance budgets cover the rest |
| **Canonical hygiene** | One URL form; self-referential absolute `rel="canonical"`; redirects are the strongest signal when a page moves; where a server redirect is impossible, a `meta refresh` of 0 seconds counts as permanent | Canonical links come with 3.5 when the web address is set. Page-rename redirects are v1.1 (G7) |
| **Favicon and site name** | A square icon of at least 48 px; `WebSite` structured data with the site name on the home page | Favicon set since 3.4; `WebSite` is one line beside `LocalBusiness` |
| **Robots and sitemaps** | robots.txt controls crawling, **not** indexing: a page both disallowed and `noindex` keeps its `noindex` unread. A sitemap matters for large or brand-new sites with no inbound links; a small, well-linked site does not need one, though it is harmless | 3.5 writes robots.txt and the sitemap. *Allow indexing* off must mean `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` on every page, not a `Disallow` |

**What Google says has no effect** (and the builders still sell): the meta keywords tag ("no effect on indexing and ranking at all"; Bing once called it a spam signal); hard title and description character limits; keyword density and "focus keyword" scores; heading order as a ranking matter (it is an accessibility matter); FAQ rich results (retired 7 May 2026); sitelinks search box markup (retired Nov 2024); star ratings on your own business (ineligible, and a manual-action risk); breadcrumb markup on a flat site; hreflang on a one-language site; city pages ("Plumber in ‹suburb›" × 30: a doorway-page spam policy); and the whole "AI visibility" layer. On the last: Google's AI optimisation guide (2026) says no new files, markup or Markdown copies are needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, that Google Search ignores `llms.txt` ("neither harm nor help"), and John Mueller said in June 2025 that no AI system used it; Bing's guidelines (rewritten Feb 2026) say the same things that help ordinary search help Copilot grounding: plain facts near the top, one topic per page, consistent names.

**Patterns.** Every one of the eight has a per-page title, description and slug, usually with a search-result preview and a soft character guide; a way to hide a page from search; a social image; an automatic sitemap and robots.txt; automatic WebP and lazy loading sold as "we handle speed"; a Search Console hook; and, since 2025, an AI button that drafts the title and description. Three (Wix, Squarespace, Durable) write LocalBusiness or Organization JSON-LD from the business profile without the owner seeing it. The onboarding shape is a checklist that starts from business name, location and a few keywords and turns them into tasks. Only Wix and Webflow redirect automatically on rename; only they let you edit robots.txt or the structured data.

Blocs is the strongest for a static export (robots and canonical checkboxes, social cards with a fallback, WebP with fallbacks, AI descriptions); Elements is the closest to Webforge's output list (sitemap, robots, `llms.txt`, Markdown twins, favicons).

## 4. Where Webforge stands

**Built** (merged by 28 Oct 2026):
- Site Settings › *Search engines*: *Allow indexing* and *Include a sitemap*; the site's name, description, language and web address (1.17, X3).
- Page Settings: a title "shown in the browser tab and search results", a description with the site's as fallback, a share image with the site's as fallback, *Hide from navigation* (1.18).
- The exported head: charset, viewport, `lang`, `<title>` as *Page | Site name*, `<meta name="description">`, the favicon set (3.3, 3.4). Pictures as above (§2).
- Site Check (3.6): page titles missing (*Should fix*, an error on publish), the web address missing (the same), alt text, broken links, missing images, placeholder text, large images, large files, contrast, underlined text, device names, the home page, and forms.

**Merged the same morning.** 3.5 ([#63](https://github.com/nexustech/webforge/issues/63), [#548](https://github.com/nexustech/webforge/pull/548), 1.5 actual days): sitemap.xml with canonical links and absolute Open Graph URLs when the web address is set, robots.txt, the web manifest, Open Graph and Twitter tags, `noindex` on every page when *Let search engines show the site* is off (and `Disallow: /` in robots.txt with it), 404.html, `{year}`, and `llms.txt` with Markdown copies behind *Help AI tools read your site*, on for new sites and off for existing ones (F-EX-5, MVP §7 item 12's proposed default). v1.1: page-rename redirects (G7), a social share preview in Page Settings (G22), AI-written descriptions and alt text with the person's own account (G25b). v1.2+: multilingual (M4), which is where hreflang belongs.

**Gaps**, against §2 and §3:
1. **No structured data.** The one output that three hosted builders write silently from the business profile, and that Google documents a rich result for, is absent, although every field it needs is in Site Settings and the templates show the hours and address on the page already.
2. **No way to hide one page from search.** *Allow indexing* is site-wide; *Hide from navigation* is a menu matter. Every competitor has the per-page switch.
3. **No search-result preview** and no sign of how long is too long. Every hosted builder and Bootstrap Studio show one.
4. **Site Check reads as quality control**, not as "will people find this": no rule for a missing description, duplicate titles, a page without a main heading, or a business with no address or hours; no pointer to the Business Profile or Search Console, the two actions that move a local business most.
5. **Turning indexing off writes both `noindex` and `Disallow: /`**, and the `Disallow` is the line Google says hides the `noindex`: a crawler that may not fetch the page never reads the tag, so the page can stay indexed from its links alone.
6. **`llms.txt` is filed under *Search engines* and on by default for new sites**, while Google's documented position since 2026 is that it has no effect on search; MVP §7 item 12 was still open when 3.5 shipped the proposed default.

Everything else on Google's list Webforge already does by construction, and better than most of the hosted builders' exports: flat HTML, real links, responsive pictures with dimensions and lazy loading, one small stylesheet, no third-party scripts.

## 5. Proposals

The thread through them: generate the mechanics from what the owner has already typed, say in plain words what each thing does for them, and never add a control that Google documents as having no effect.

### 5.1 The exported site

| ID | Proposal | Effort | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEO-1 | **Business details as structured data.** The home page (and the Contact page, when there is one) carries a JSON-LD `LocalBusiness` built from Site Settings: name, description, web address, telephone, the postal address, opening hours (as `openingHoursSpecification`; closed days and the note handled per Google's rules), the share image, and the social profiles as `sameAs`; plus a `WebSite` object with the site name. **The business type picks the subtype**: the quick start's business step (QS-1, 3.11) already asks it; it becomes a Site Settings field with the same list, mapped to schema.org types (café → `CafeOrCoffeeShop`, restaurant → `Restaurant` with `servesCuisine`, and `menu` pointing at the page with a Price list section, salon → `HairSalon` or `BeautySalon`, trades → `Plumber`, `Electrician`, `HomeAndConstructionBusiness`, consultant → `ProfessionalService`, shop → `Store`, otherwise `LocalBusiness`). Only what the page shows is written (Google's rule), so the hours and address appear only when a bound component shows them; never `aggregateRating` or `review`. A `priceRange` and `geo` are optional fields in *Contact details*. Readable JSON, validated against zod, golden files per template; a Site Check tip when the type is set but the address or hours are empty | 2 | MVP, 3.5 (+2 days) or its own item |
| SEO-2 | ***Hide this page from search engines*** in Page Settings: `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` on that page, left out of the sitemap, the Markdown copies and `llms.txt`, still linked and crawlable. Its relation to *Hide from navigation* said in the hint (one is the menu, the other is Google). The 404 page is always `noindex` | 0.5 | MVP, 3.5 |
| SEO-3 | **Rules for the search-engine switches** (no new code beyond 3.5): *Allow indexing* off writes `noindex` on every page and leaves robots.txt allowing crawling (a `Disallow` would hide the `noindex`); robots.txt otherwise allows everything and names the sitemap; the sitemap lists canonical URLs with `lastmod` from the page's last change and leaves out hidden and 404 pages; canonical links are absolute and self-referential; no `<meta name="keywords">`, ever; no hreflang while a site has one language. | SEO-4 | **Preload the first picture.** The hero's image gets `fetchpriority="high"` (and a `<link rel="preload">` when the pipeline knows the chosen source); everything else stays lazy.
