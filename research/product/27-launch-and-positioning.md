# Launch and positioning: what Webforge says it is, and how owners hear of it

**Status:** ✅ Decided 2026-11-27: LP-1 and LP-3 to LP-9 adopted, LP-3 for the website only and LP-9 at v1.1; LP-2 and LP-10 not adopted ([§8](#8-decisions))
**Date:** 2026-11-27
**Question:** The product, its price and its licence are decided. What should Webforge say it is, to whom, and where; what must its own website hold before Paddle will approve it; and how should it be launched?

**Short answer:** Say what no rival says to a small-business owner: you pay once and every later version is included, the site is yours and private, and here is where it lives and what that costs. Don't lead with "no code" or AI, which everyone claims. Build the website in three steps: a waiting list now, the pricing and legal pages before the application to Paddle, the checkout at v1.1. Launch slowly, through search and other people's pages, and not on a launch day: the first-hand accounts found show launch days selling almost nothing for apps like this. The free v1.0 is the time to earn Windows' trust in the installer and the first mentions elsewhere. Two points need a solicitor before the pricing page is written.

## 1. What is decided already

- **Who it is for:** Sam, who owns a small café, salon or trades business, is comfortable with Canva and Word, dislikes monthly builder fees and doesn't know what HTML or hosting are ([MVP spec §1](../../spec/02-mvp-spec.md#1-product-goal)).
- **The selling points:** the one-off licence and the complete export (WF-6, [competitors/01](../competitors/01-webflow.md)); "your files, one price, nothing lost, AI only if you turn it on" (UC-5, [competitors/09](../competitors/09-what-users-complain-about.md)).
- **The licence and who takes the money:** SL-1 to SL-10 ([product/26](26-licensing-and-selling.md)): US $99 before tax, every later version included, three computers, a 14-day trial, a 30-day refund, Paddle as the seller of record. v1.0 is free; selling starts at v1.1 (PH6-2).
- **No price in the app or its help** (product owner, 27 Nov 2026). Prices and terms live on the website and at the checkout, so the website is where this note's wording matters.
- **The website is a lead-time item** in the plan ([§4](../../spec/04-implementation-plan.md)): Paddle reviews it before approving a seller. It has no issue.
- **A five-page outline exists** from the product owner's trial of building the website in Webforge ([`docs/testing/own-website-with-claude-code.md`](../../docs/testing/own-website-with-claude-code.md)): Home, Features, Compare, Pricing and Download.
- **Beta testers** come from a waiting-list page and email (Q7), recruited from item 5.7 (mid-December 2026).

## 2. Method and limits

- **Seventeen products' own pages,** read on 27 Nov 2026: the home and pricing pages of nine desktop builders (Bootstrap Studio, Pinegrow, Blocs, RapidWeaver Elements, Mobirise, Sitely, Nicepage, Publii, WebSite X5) and eight hosted ones (Wix, Squarespace, Hostinger, GoDaddy, Jimdo, Durable, Carrd, Webnode). GoDaddy's pages and Wix's plans page could not be read. The pages were read through a tool that summarises, so no wording here is a quotation, and Black Friday prices were showing on several.
- **Launch accounts and studies,** 2016 to 2026: makers' own write-ups of launching desktop and one-time-purchase apps, and surveys of small businesses and software buyers. Several Indie Hackers posts could be read only as search excerpts; §4 marks them.
- **Primary sources for the website's duties:** Paddle's and Stripe's help pages, UK legislation, the ICO, the CMA, the ASA's CAP Code, Microsoft and Apple.
- **Not found:** a survey that asks small-business owners how they chose a website builder; public search volumes for the phrases in §4.4; first-hand numbers for YouTube reviewers, newsletters or small-business press.
- **Not legal advice.** §5 is background for a research note. Points marked _to confirm_ need a solicitor or an accountant.
- Nothing was run, signed up for or submitted.

### 3.4 The question nobody answers: where does my site live?

One-time sellers handle hosting in one of three ways: include a year of it (WebSite X5, Bootstrap Studio, Mobirise's free address), say "publish to your own hosting" and stop (Elements, Blocs, Sitely), or list technical destinations (Publii, Pinegrow). None of the pages read explains to a beginner what hosting and a domain are, what they cost, or how to choose. Squarespace says the opposite, that no separate hosting is needed, and no desktop rival answers it. In one survey of 201 US owners, setting up the domain and hosting was the task they were least willing to hand to AI (§4.1). This is the clearest gap, and Sam is the person it stops.

### 3.5 What buyers resent in the marketing

- **"Lifetime" or "one-time" that turns out to end.** Nicepage's reviewers call its lifetime licence misleading because updates stop after a year; Mobirise's say access went from for good to a year; a reviewer reportedly found Mobirise's yearly bundle looked like a single payment.
- **A low price that rises.** Hostinger's and GoDaddy's first-term prices against their renewals; Elements' rise from $59 to $99 during its beta.
- **Costs shown late.** A custom-domain fee disclosed only at publishing (Mobirise, as reported).

These match what [competitors/09](../competitors/09-what-users-complain-about.md) found in forums. A pay-once claim is believed only if it says plainly what happens to updates.

## 4. How apps like this are launched

### 4.1 Where owners look

- **No study of how owners choose a builder was found.** The nearest evidence is about software buyers in general.
- **Few owners build their own site.** Of 406 US small-business owners (Clutch, Aug 2025), 9 % built theirs themselves; 45 % used an agency, 37 % someone in-house, 9 % a freelancer. Of the 17 % with no site, 16 % gave cost as the reason. This bears on who Webforge reaches first, and on the freelancer the owner already knows.
- **AI assistants are where research now starts** for many buyers: 51 % of 1,076 business software buyers start with a chatbot more often than with Google (G2, Mar 2026, not split by size of business). Surveys of owners' own use of AI range from 24 % to 74 %.
- **Younger UK owners** turn to YouTube (51 %), LinkedIn (41 %) and Facebook or Instagram (37 %) for business guidance (Worldpay, read as an excerpt only).

### 4.3 Order and timing

- **A waiting list converts if people aren't kept waiting:** about 20 % when access comes within a month, under 10 % after three (Lenny Rachitsky, 2022, cited second-hand). Two makers report 300 sign-ups and nought or three buyers.
- **Building in public reaches other makers, not buyers,** by several makers' accounts.
- **Pages comparing a product with a named rival** drew steady visits for one company (about 5,500 a month across 37 pages); no evidence of what they sold.
- **Launch day against a slow launch:** no controlled evidence. The accounts in §4.2 favour steady search and writing over a day.
- **An unsigned installer loses people:** one maker estimates 15 to 20 % at the first security warning. Microsoft says a signed new app still warns until it has a reputation, which can take weeks and hundreds of clean installs, and that a dearer certificate no longer skips this.

### 4.4 Search

- **No public volumes** for "website builder without subscription", "one-time purchase website builder", "offline website builder", "website builder no monthly fee" or "desktop website builder". A keyword tool would give them.
- **Who answers those searches today:** thin lists of "best offline website builders", directory filters, and rivals' own landing pages (Nicepage, Realmac, Bootstrap Studio). The lists name Mobirise, RapidWeaver, Blocs, Nicepage, WebSite X5, Pinegrow, Bootstrap Studio and a few others.
- **A reading, not a measurement:** the broad phrases belong to Wix and the large publishers; these narrow ones could be matched by a page that answers the question well.

### 4.5 AI assistants

- **They name few products:** in a study of over a million shopping questions, three quarters of recommendations went to about the same five brands.
- **They draw on other people's pages:** reviews and editorial 34 %, forums 22 %, marketplaces 18 %, the brand's own site 14 %, video 7 % (the same study). Visibility goes with mentions on YouTube and across the web far more than with links (Ahrefs, 75,000 brands; a correlation only).
- **A file written for them (`llms.txt`) shows no effect** across about 300,000 domains.
- So being listed, reviewed and shown in videos by others matters more than anything on Webforge's own website.

### 4.6 What goes wrong

Attention on a launch day that doesn't turn into sales; an audience of makers; press with nothing after it; a price set too low; an unsigned build; launch wording written the night before; and a lifetime-deal contract.

## 5. What the website must hold

### 5.1 For Paddle

- **A live site, served over HTTPS,** that describes the product and its main features, with a pricing page. Paddle accepts a screenshot of a pricing page that isn't live yet.
- **Terms, a refund policy and a privacy policy,** reachable from the navigation and not behind a login, carrying the company's name or a sole trader's legal name. The name must match the Paddle account.
- **The terms say who sells:** that orders are handled by Paddle as the reseller and merchant of record. Paddle's own drafting guide calls leaving this out the commonest reason for rejection.
- **The refund policy** gives a window of 14 to 90 days without conditions, and sends requests through Paddle. SL-6's 30 days fits.
- **Review:** most are approved automatically; a manual review is estimated at five to seven working days. One maker's account has three rounds over two weeks, for a refund policy with conditions and a name that didn't match.
- **Not confirmed:** whether a site passes before the product can be downloaded or bought. v1.0's free download would be there by then.
- **Stripe Managed Payments,** the second choice: open to UK businesses and to downloadable software; no comparable list of website requirements was found.

### 5.2 What stays Webforge's

Paddle makes the sale, takes the tax and pays refunds. The licence agreement, the product's support, the website's claims, and the privacy notice for the website, the mailing list and the app stay with Webforge. Paddle's own policy gives buyers 14 days; the 30 days of SL-6 are Webforge's promise, kept by refunding through Paddle. _To confirm:_ how Paddle treats a request made to it between day 15 and day 30.

### 5.3 Consumer law, as it shapes the wording

- **Before the sale,** a UK or EU consumer must be told the product's main characteristics, the trader's name and address, the total price with taxes, how to cancel, and what the download needs to run. So **what the computer needs is a legal item,** not only good practice. _To confirm:_ how these duties divide between Paddle and Webforge; the safe course is to give all of it on the website.
- **The price.** UK law (Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, s. 230) and the advertising code require the total price, taxes included, where it can be worked out. **A bare "US $99 plus tax" shown to a UK or EU visitor is therefore at risk.** $99 before tax stays the price (product owner, 27 Nov 2026); what changes is how the page shows it. Paddle can supply a price with tax for the visitor's country. _To confirm with a solicitor: this is the main point._
- **Cancelling.** A download may start inside the 14 days only if the buyer agrees and accepts the right to cancel is lost; Paddle's checkout terms already say so. The 30-day refund sits on top and must be described without conditions.
- **Reviews and testimonials:** invented ones and hidden rewards are banned outright; a site that shows reviews must take steps to check they are real.

### 5.4 Privacy and email

- **The privacy notice** names who is responsible and how to reach them, what is collected and why, on what basis, who receives it (the email service, the host, Paddle), how long it is kept, the person's rights, and how to complain to the ICO.
- **A waiting list needs consent:** an unticked box or a plain statement at sign-up, a record of it, and a way out in every message. The exemption for existing customers does not cover people who haven't bought. Sole traders count as individuals. _To confirm:_ whether someone who downloaded the trial may be emailed without consent.
- **Cookies:** a site that stores nothing on the visitor's device needs no banner. Analytics that count visits without identifying anyone now have an exemption with conditions.
- **Not researched:** registration with the ICO and its fee. _To confirm._

### 5.5 The download page

- What the computer needs (the Windows and macOS versions and the kind of Mac), the file's size, the version and its date, a link to what changed, and the name of the publisher that the security dialog will show.
- **Never suggest turning protection off.** Microsoft's advice is to sign every release with one identity and tell early users they may be asked, and to go on only when the publisher's name is right.
- A notarised Mac app shows only the usual first-open question.
- Publishing checksums is common; signing does the real work.

### 5.6 Claims

- **Hold evidence** for every claim of fact.
- **A comparison with named rivals** must compare like with like on features that matter and can be checked, and say where to check. So the Compare page gives its date and links each rival's own page; [product/10](10-competitor-pricing.md) already records the prices and when they were read. _To confirm:_ the use of rivals' names and marks.
- **"Free trial" and "money back" are different things** and must be worded apart.
- **"Lifetime" and "pay once, use for good" have been ruled misleading** where the seller couldn't support them. Say what the licence covers: every later version, three computers, one person.

## 6. What follows for Webforge

1. **The ground to stand on is open.** No desktop builder speaks to a small-business owner by trade, none but Publii speaks of privacy, and none answers "where does my site live?". Webforge's decided licence is also plainer than its rivals': every later version, with no year of updates and no tiers.
2. **The website does three jobs at three times,** and the first is due now: a waiting list for the beta (5.7, mid-December), the pricing and legal pages for Paddle's review (before 6.0b), and the checkout (v1.1).
3. **The free v1.0 is worth more than goodwill.** It is when the installer earns its reputation with Windows, when testers and early owners write the first mentions elsewhere, and when search starts to find the pages. All three take weeks and can't be bought at v1.1.
4. **The first buyers may not be Sam.** Nine in ten owners have someone else build their site. The people who reach Webforge first are likelier to be the owner who already tried a builder, and the relative or freelancer who makes sites for others. [competitors/09](../competitors/09-what-users-complain-about.md) found the same people in the forums. Who the owner is stays a gap in the research.
5. **The website is built in Webforge,** as the product owner's trial began. Two things it needs are outside what an exported site does by itself: the waiting list's form (a form needs a third party, a gap §1 of competitors/09 already names) and, at v1.1, Paddle's checkout script, added in Custom Code. Both are the owner's own experience of the product, and worth writing down as they are met.
6. **One document is out of date:** the trial's outline of the Pricing page says a 7-day trial; SL-5 made it 14 days. Corrected with this note.
